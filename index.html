03 · Research

Green corrosion inhibition, predicted by machine learning

Undergraduate research project · FUTO · Feb 2025

Predicting Corrosion Inhibition of API 5L X70 Steel Pipes Immersed in Mango Leaf Extract Using Machine Learning

Pipeline steel corrodes, and many commercial inhibitors are toxic. I investigated mango leaf extract as a sustainable, plant-based corrosion inhibitor for API 5L X70 pipeline steel, combining experimental corrosion testing with statistical and machine learning models to predict how well it protects the steel.

Method
01

Extract preparation

Mango leaves processed into a plant extract and prepared at different concentrations.

02

Steel specimens

API 5L X70 pipeline steel coupons immersed in corrosive media with and without the extract.

03

Weight loss

Mass loss over time used to calculate corrosion rate and inhibition efficiency.

04

PDP and AFM

Potentiodynamic polarisation for electrochemical behaviour; atomic force microscopy for surface morphology.

05

Machine learning

Support Vector Machine and Random Forest models trained to predict inhibition performance.

My role

  • Lead student researcher for a 12-member team, August 2024 to February 2025.
  • Coordinated experimental design, data analysis, technical documentation and engagement with supervisors.
  • The team achieved a 90% distinction rate.

Why it matters

  • Pipeline integrity is central to safe oil and gas operations in Nigeria and beyond.
  • Plant-based inhibitors are low-cost, locally available and less toxic.
  • Machine learning can cut the number of costly laboratory tests needed to screen inhibitors.
